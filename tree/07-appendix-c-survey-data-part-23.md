# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 23 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

Page 77 contains survey responses from Oregon business owners, workers, and community leaders identifying tax burdens, regulations, education outcomes, and sectoral gaps as key competitiveness challenges. Dominant themes: payroll/income taxes and licensing fees are prohibitive; child care, food manufacturing workforce, rural development, and arts/culture infrastructure need state investment; peer states (Texas, Florida, Washington, Kentucky, Michigan) offer models for tax reform, employer partnerships, and targeted subsidies. Many respondents frame economic competitiveness against a perceived business-unfriendly regulatory and tax environment.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Child care identified as critical economic infrastructure requiring statewide coordination across public investment, employer partnerships, and supply expansion, especially for infant/toddler care; respondent cites Michigan Tri-Share and Vermont payroll-contribution models. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Multiple small business respondents cite payroll taxes, income taxes, licensing fees, and workers' compensation as prohibitive costs; some propose replacing income taxes with sales tax modeled on Washington state. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Food and beverage manufacturers report mid-scale processing gap: companies struggle to scale from startup to regional/national production due to lack of equipment capital, technical workforce shortages, and regulatory complexity; suggest Washington State's Local Food System Infrastructure Grant model. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Oregonian business owner reports 4.4 billion in state contracting awarded out-of-state last biennium; advocates for local procurement mandates to retain jobs and tax revenue in-state. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Respondent notes SB1507 claims to bring approximately $310 million in new tax revenue, while state proposes $375 million in Trail Blazers subsidy, highlighting perceived fiscal misalignment. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Rural respondents report unequal investment distribution favoring Portland/Salem/Eugene I-5 corridor; advocate for rural-specific programs, dedicated resources, and community-defined infrastructure solutions. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- K-12 education outcomes cited as inadequate (reading, math proficiency, graduation rates); respondents advocate for school choice, standards reform, and accountability measures to build competitive workforce. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Arts and cultural infrastructure unevenly distributed west vs. east of Cascades; respondent proposes Art Park in Central Oregon (Redmond/Bend area) modeled on New York's Artpark at Lewiston, established 1974. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
- Respondents reference successful peer-state models: Texas and Florida for business climate, Washington for child care/food infrastructure grants, Mississippi education 'Miracle,' Kentucky/Michigan employer partnership models. ([p. 77](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=77))
