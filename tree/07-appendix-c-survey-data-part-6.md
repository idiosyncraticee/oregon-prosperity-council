# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 6 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

This survey response page captures 30+ individual and small-business respondents addressing business climate, workforce development, housing, energy policy, and transit. Dominant themes: workforce development system fragmentation (especially early childhood and rural economies), insufficient wage standards tied to cost of living, housing shortage requiring zoning/permitting reform, public transit funding cuts, and utility wildfire liability. Respondents split between advocates for business-friendly deregulation/lower taxes and those pushing for worker-centered policies, progressive taxation (billionaire's tax models), sectoral bargaining, and increased public investment in essential services.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Early childhood education training shortage: respondents report only ~15% of needed workforce trained to required early learning credential standards ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Portland General Electric insurance premiums increased 180% in last 5 years due to wildfire liability risk ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- PGE service area generates $4.6 billion in annual economic output and supports 9,421 jobs ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Respondents call for union apprenticeship enrollment targets of at least 25% in key sectors (healthcare, logistics, retail, construction, hospitality) ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Multiple respondents advocate preserving Multnomah County's universal preschool program as statewide model ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Rural Oregon workforce advocates identify system fragmentation across K-12, higher education, workforce boards, and economic development as core barrier ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Respondents cite transit funding cuts and freeway expansion (Interstate Bridge Replacement, Rose Quarter) as contradictions to economic development goals ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
- Multiple respondents propose billionaire's tax modeled on Massachusetts and California examples ([p. 60](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=60))
