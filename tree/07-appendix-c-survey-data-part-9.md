# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 9 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

This appendix presents raw survey responses from individual Oregonians and organizational representatives on state prosperity priorities. Respondents identify workforce development (especially preschool and K-12 education), small business support, affordable housing, clean energy transition, healthcare workforce shortages, forest protection, and water management as critical challenges. Most advocate for increased public investment in education and childcare, stronger environmental protections, progressive taxation, and streamlined permitting processes.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Universal, free preschool for all Oregon children is advocated across multiple responses, citing 3-to-1 return on investment and 22% family income increases ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- K-12 education funding inadequacy is frequently cited, with concerns about reading and math proficiency compared to other states ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Small business support versus large corporation subsidies is highlighted; small businesses comprise 90% of Oregon's businesses ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Clean energy transition barriers identified include limited electricity capacity, complex permitting, and transmission infrastructure needs ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Healthcare workforce shortages in nursing and long-term care are cited with calls for staffing mandates and training programs ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Homelessness is identified as harming tourism and small businesses; respondents advocate housing-first policies and addiction treatment ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Water use priorities: irrigated agriculture consumes 80% of diversions but provides 1.5% of GDP, while water-dependent businesses provide 30% of GDP with 6% of diversions ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Forest protection from logging and clear-cutting advocated for tourism, climate, and habitat protection; concerns about federal land privatization ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))
- Permitting complexity cited as barrier to business and real estate development; uniform permitting processes proposed ([p. 63](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=63))

Amounts: 6% of all water diversions · 30% of Oregon's GDP · 80% of all water diversions · 1.5% of Oregon's GDP · 90% of the businesses · $16 billion in annual consumer spending · 192,000 full- and part-time jobs · $14.8 billion in damages · Dates/FTE: 2027 · 2023 · 1980 · 2025 · Programs: Preschool for All · HB 2021 · Climate Protection Program · Clean Fuels Program · CREP · Groundwater Management Areas · Parties: Tina Kotek · Keith Wilson · Legislature · ODOT
