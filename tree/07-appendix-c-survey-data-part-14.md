# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 14 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: high)*

Individual Oregonians (residents and small businesses) submitted survey responses on economic competitiveness. Dominant themes: strong support for expanding Preschool for All as workforce investment; divergent views on business regulation (some want deregulation citing Idaho/Florida/Texas models; others want higher corporate/wealth taxation); concern about data center tax incentives and environmental impacts; emphasis on education funding, childcare access, workforce training, and affordable housing as keys to prosperity.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Preschool for All cited repeatedly by respondents as critical workforce and economic tool, with claims of 3-to-1 return on investment and raising family incomes by approximately 22% on average. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Data centers mentioned frequently as controversial: respondents question tax breaks, environmental impacts (water/land/energy use), and call for moratorium until transparency requirements met. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Respondents invoke contrasting models: those opposing regulation cite Florida, Texas, Idaho as low-tax/low-regulation examples; those favoring progressive policy cite New York, New Mexico, Nordic countries. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Workforce development emphasized through education investment, union labor advocacy, living wages, apprenticeships, teacher compensation, and childcare access to enable labor force participation. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Tax policy split: some respondents advocate reduced business/personal taxes; others advocate higher taxes on wealthy and corporations to fund education, housing, childcare, and public services. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Housing affordability, zoning reform (ADUs, middle housing, urban density), and property tax system overhaul (overturning Measure 5 and Measure 50) cited as tools to increase housing supply and infrastructure funding. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
- Environmental transition priorities: clean energy, EV infrastructure, public transit, building codes, restrictions on fossil fuel expansion and data center sprawl on farmland. ([p. 68](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=68))
