# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 20 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

Page 74 captures survey responses from Oregon residents and business representatives on economic competitiveness. Dominant themes: excessive tax and regulatory burden deter business formation; small businesses (82% of economy) need support; K-12 education ranked 41st-47th requires increased investment; Climate Protection Program threatens energy jobs and affordability; construction workforce shortage; agricultural land loss accelerating (120K-140K acres/year); rural communities neglected. Respondents advocate: consolidate state portals, streamline permitting, reform taxes (add sales tax, reduce income tax), protect farmland, enact worker-centered energy policy.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Small businesses represent 82% of Oregon's economy but face disproportionate regulatory burden; respondents cite need to consolidate multiple state websites into single portal with one login ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- K-12 education ranking 41st-47th nationally; respondents call for legislative priority and investment, with one noting ranking improves to 24th when funding increases ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Climate Protection Program identified as highest carbon pricing in North America; union workers and energy employees cite $4.9 Billion cost to natural gas consumers over 2025-2035 period and threat to family-wage jobs ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Healthcare costs barrier for small businesses; respondents demand parity with large employers or regulation preventing tiered health plan offerings ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Oregon losing 120,000-140,000 acres agricultural land annually; landowners call for rezoning protections and equal voice in land-use decisions alongside data centers and solar/wind development ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Construction workforce shortage: apprentice-to-journeyman ratio needs increase from 1:1 to 1:2 to accelerate training capacity ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- January 2026 QSBS tax policy change (revocation) called devastating to startup ecosystem; Oregon only state taking 10% of startup sale earnings ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Untapped talent pool (people with disabilities, reentry workers, justice-involved individuals) identified; respondents propose hiring incentives and recognition programs modeled on Missouri Inclusion Award ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Rural communities feel neglected by Salem; respondents propose rural-oriented Prosperity Council offshoot to address local workforce retention and economic development ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
- Tax structure disparity: 48 states have sales taxes; respondents call for Oregon to implement sales tax and reduce income tax to improve competitiveness and capture tourism revenue ([p. 74](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=74))
