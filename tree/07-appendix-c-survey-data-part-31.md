# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 31 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

Diverse Oregon respondents—nonprofits, small businesses, individuals—identify workforce shortages and skill gaps as the dominant challenge, compounded by childcare and housing costs, excessive regulation, and insufficient arts/culture funding. Respondents advocate for business incentives and streamlined permitting to compete with Idaho/Utah/Texas, integrated education-workforce-community learning systems with flexible funding, dedicated arts/culture revenue mechanisms, and regional balance beyond I-5 corridors.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Sean Andries (Chehalem Cultural Center): Oregon ranks 'near the bottom in terms of State level support to Art and Culture per capita'; Oregon Cultural Trust tax credit has 'not kept up with inflation' ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Rob Freres (Freres Lumber): 400-employee company reports 20 workers absent daily; 40,000 hours missed annually; calls for elimination of DEQ, OSHA, BOLI ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Cheryl Viola (Pendleton Chamber of Commerce): Idaho and Utah 'very good at attracting businesses'; calls for business retention focus, workforce development, and permitting improvements ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Quinn Hanson (G.A. Rogers): Central Oregon needs '6-12 legit employers for every given sector'; Texas, Florida, Idaho 'attracting the majority of new investments' ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Allison French (Talent Maker City): Education and workforce systems 'operate in silos' with rigid funding; RISE internship program with Rogue Workforce Partnership demonstrates community-based model promise ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Elizabeth Farrar Campbell: All 36 Oregon counties should have at least '50% access to preschool'; references Preschool Promise and OPK programs; cites HB 2727 DLCD childcare planning recommendations ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Heather McNeill: Construction workforce shortage drives up costs and timelines; average worker age needs to drop 'from being in their 50s to their 30s' ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
- Lianne Thompson: Calls for overhauling 'legacy systems of taxation, land use, and permitting'; seeks balanced 'urban-rural approach' beyond I-5 population centers ([p. 85](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=85))
