# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 8 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: medium)*

Survey respondents across diverse backgrounds prioritize: universal Preschool for All and childcare access as workforce infrastructure; higher wages, union protections, and labor enforcement; progressive taxation (wealth, capital gains, income taxes on high earners) to fund public goods; affordable housing including public/social housing models; environmental protections especially limiting data-center incentives; and robust K-12 and higher-education funding. Some respondents call for lower business taxes and lighter regulation. One respondent critiques the Prosperity Council's composition as undemocratic and under-representative of workers and communities.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- Preschool for All program cited repeatedly as essential public infrastructure for workforce development, with documented 3-to-1 return on investment and family income gains ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Respondents call for higher minimum wages, union triggers, and enforcement of labor laws; one proposes doubling Oregon union density from ~16% to 32% ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Progressive taxation proposals include wealth taxes, capital-gains taxes, and higher income-tax rates on high earners to fund childcare, education, and public housing ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Multiple respondents oppose tax incentives for data centers and call for moratoria on discretionary corporate tax breaks; SB 1507 (QSBS removal) criticized for discouraging startups ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- K-12 and higher-education funding increases urged as critical workforce development; respondents note underfunding at PSU, SOU, PCC causing budget crises ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Housing affordability identified as major barrier; proposals include public/social housing (Seattle and Maryland models cited), rent control, and infill zoning ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Paid Leave Oregon and universal single-payer healthcare cited as successful worker-support models; one respondent cites Oregon's #1 Oxfam ranking on worker-friendly policy ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- One detailed response proposes converting Oregon economy toward worker cooperatives and democratic economic governance, citing Quebec, Colorado, and other state models ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Respondent explicitly critiques Prosperity Council composition as undemocratic, citing unequal representation of workers, labor unions, and marginalized communities vs. business leaders ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
- Environmental protection priorities include strict regulations on data-center water/energy use, protection of farmland and forests, and opposition to new refineries ([p. 62](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=62))
