# Appendix C: Survey Data -- Part 27 of 31

<!-- enrich:begin -->

## TL;DR  *(generated · confidence: high)*

Survey respondents on page 81 express deep concerns about Oregon's business climate, citing high taxes (CAT, income tax, gross revenue proposals), regulatory burden, and permitting delays as primary barriers to business development and job creation. Workforce development emerges as critical, with calls for K-12 education reform, industry-aligned training programs, and child care expansion. Regional economic strategies (citing Research Triangle model) and infrastructure investment are advocated. Some respondents prioritize environmental protection, while others emphasize tax reduction and deregulation as paths to competitiveness.

**Key points** *(each cites a PDF page)*:

- High taxes repeatedly cited as barrier: CAT, income tax, metro tax, gross revenue taxes, and proposals for additional levies on business and individuals ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Permitting delays and land-use regulations prevent industrial and commercial development; respondents call for streamlined, predictable processes under 30 days ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- K-12 education system needs improvement in reading, math, achievement standards, and attendance requirements (85% minimum proposed) ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Workforce development through local boards and employer partnerships aligned with industry needs; Prosperity 10K and regional programs cited as proven models ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Child care shortages prevent workforce participation; respondents call for universal child care and expanded ERDC funding similar to K-12 education ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Portland revitalization requires clean streets, street lighting, safety infrastructure, and public gathering spaces to attract business and residents ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Regional economic development strategies modeled on Research Triangle (North Carolina) advocated; mid-sized cities like Corvallis need targeted support ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
- Environmental protection advocates call for stronger state regulations, increased DEQ and ODFW funding, and protection against pollution and sprawl ([p. 81](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/Oregon%20Prosperity%20Council%20Report_June%202026.pdf#page=81))
